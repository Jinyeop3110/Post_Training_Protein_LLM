Resume Story: Two Crashes, One Completion

Training did not run in a single shot. Here is the timeline:

Mar 7 — Initial launch

Started from scratch on 4.89M dataset. Trained smoothly to step 8250.

Step 8250 — Crash #1: CPU OOM

Dataloader replay on resume attempted to re-iterate through all 8250 steps of data to restore sampler state. On a 4.89M dataset, this caused CPU memory exhaustion (>200GB RSS). The HuggingFace Trainer's data_skip mechanism is not designed for datasets this large.

Restart with ignore_data_skip=True

Bypassed dataloader replay by setting ignore_data_skip=True in TrainingArguments. This means some samples from the first 8250 steps may be revisited, but for a 1-epoch run this is acceptable. Training resumed and continued from step 8250.

Steps 8250 → 9647 — Training complete

Ran the remaining ~1400 steps without issues. Checkpoints saved at 9250, 9500, and 9647 (final). Training itself completed successfully.

Step 9647 — Crash #2: NCCL timeout

Post-training evaluation callback attempted FSDP generation, which triggered an NCCL timeout across ranks. This is the same known FSDP generation issue that affects all our distributed runs. The crash occurred after the final checkpoint was saved, so no training data was lost.

Sampler state save/restore is a known gap. The ignore_data_skip workaround means the last ~1400 steps may have seen some duplicate data. For a full fix, see the pending task to implement sampler state save/restore for checkpoint resume.

Key Takeaways

Text-only SFT works. Even without ESM-3 structural embeddings, raw amino acid sequences + LoRA fine-tuning achieves BLEU 0.257 and ROUGE-L 0.432 on protein understanding tasks. The text approach has a clear advantage on catalytic activity prediction (BLEU 0.367 vs MLP's 0.313), suggesting that raw sequence tokens carry complementary information to structural embeddings.

But MLP is stronger overall. ESM-3 embeddings give a +57% ROUGE-L advantage on function descriptions and produce more concise, less hallucinatory outputs. The text model tends to generate verbose, cascading responses when uncertain — a qualitative gap that metrics alone don't fully capture.
