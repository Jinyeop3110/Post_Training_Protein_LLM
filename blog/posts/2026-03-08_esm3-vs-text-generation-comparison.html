Key Finding

ESM-3 embeddings produce measurably better generations than raw text input. At step 5500 on the same evaluation samples, ESM-3+MLP leads by +0.048 BLEU and +0.034 ROUGE-L overall. The gap is largest on function descriptions (BLEU 0.421 vs 0.235 — nearly 2x), where structural embeddings capture nuances that raw sequence tokens miss. Text-only responses are also prone to verbose hallucinations on unfamiliar proteins.

What the categories reveal

The function category is where ESM-3 embeddings shine brightest. Describing what a protein does requires understanding its 3D fold, binding interfaces, and evolutionary context — exactly the kind of information ESM-3 was trained to capture. The nearly 2x BLEU advantage (0.421 vs 0.235) is the strongest signal in this comparison.

The catalytic category is the only one where text holds a slight edge. Catalytic activity descriptions often involve specific substrate names and reaction equations that appear verbatim in training data — here, memorizing the raw sequence-to-answer mapping may be more direct than decoding from compressed embeddings.

Why ESM-3 Wins on Function

The function category gap (BLEU 0.421 vs 0.235) is the headline result. Three factors explain it:

  1. Structural encoding: ESM-3 was pre-trained on protein structure and evolution. Function often follows from fold, and the embedding captures fold-level features that raw amino-acid tokens do not.
  2. Information density: 32 attention-pooled tokens distill the sequence into a compact representation focused on biologically relevant features. The text model sees hundreds of tokens (one per residue) that are locally informative but require the LLM to do its own "encoding" at training time.
  3. Generalization to rare proteins: For well-characterized proteins (protamine, ribosomal proteins), both approaches succeed because the LLM memorized the sequence-to-function mapping. For less common proteins (RNase E modulators, membrane insertases), ESM-3's structural features provide signal that the raw sequence does not.

Why Text Wins on Catalytic

Text's slight edge on catalytic activity (0.215 vs 0.199 BLEU) likely reflects a different dynamic:

  • Catalytic reactions are often described with exact chemical formulas (e.g., "dITP + H2O = dIMP + diphosphate") that appear verbatim in training data
  • The LLM can memorize these sequence-to-equation mappings directly when it sees the raw amino acids
  • ESM-3 embeddings may compress away the fine-grained active-site residue information that distinguishes one hydrolase from another

However, the gap is small (+0.016 BLEU for text) — well within noise for 10 samples per category.

The Hallucination Problem

Beyond the aggregate scores, the most striking qualitative difference is in failure modes. When uncertain:

  • ESM-3 tends to produce short, wrong answers ("Monomer" instead of "Homodimer")
  • Text tends to produce long, hallucinated paragraphs (a paragraph about FtsZ when the protein has nothing to do with cell division)

This matters for downstream applications. A short wrong answer is easy to flag and discard. A long, confident, detailed hallucination is dangerous — it looks authoritative and could mislead researchers.

The embedding bottleneck that slightly hurts ESM-3's recall also acts as a natural regularizer against hallucination. The model cannot freely associate from raw sequence patterns to memorized but irrelevant text.

What Both Get Right

Both approaches perfectly identify several well-characterized protein families:

  • 50S ribosomal protein L6 (uL18 family)
  • Fis family transcriptional regulators
  • Protamine function (histone replacement in spermatids)
  • Common enzymatic reactions (dITP hydrolysis, nucleotide metabolism)

This shared success set represents the "easy" portion of protein understanding where sequence identity alone is sufficient for correct answers.

Conclusions and Next Steps

Verdict: ESM-3 embeddings provide a meaningful advantage over raw text input for protein understanding tasks, especially for function prediction on less common proteins. The advantage is most pronounced where structural context matters most.

  1. ESM-3+MLP is the better SFT base for GRPO: The stronger function prediction and reduced hallucination make it a better starting point for RL fine-tuning
  2. Catalytic tasks may benefit from hybrid input: Consider a combined approach that feeds both embeddings and raw sequence tokens to the LLM
  3. Hallucination control: Text-only models need explicit anti-hallucination mechanisms (e.g., calibrated uncertainty, shorter max generation length)
  4. Perceiver comparison needed: Does the Perceiver Resampler (which replaces the attention pooling + MLP with learned cross-attention) close the catalytic gap while maintaining the function advantage?
